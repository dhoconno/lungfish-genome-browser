## Summary

The most common failure was a term used with no gloss at first mention, from residue and progressive method to byte-identical and parsimony, spread across the Settings and command-line sections alike. The second most common failure was a location problem, where a control, panel, or matrix that the chapter relies on was never tied to a menu path, keystroke, or screen position a beginner could follow. A third recurring failure was an internal inconsistency, such as two command names, two stated defaults, or two flag names for one control, presented without saying which one actually governs the reader's own action.
