The one thing I learned. An alignment is a rectangle whose columns are guesses about shared ancestry, and gaps exist only to keep the rectangle square, which is why a column count near the longest input is the sign of a healthy run.

The one thing I still could not do. Produce the pairwise identity matrix, which the chapter calls the clearest single check, because the only route it gives me is the terminal.

The sentence I liked most. "An alignment that says otherwise is telling you something went wrong with the run, not something new about primates."
