# Reader report: Mapping Reads to a Reference

Reader persona: undergraduate who used Geneious in one class. Never opened a terminal.

| Location | What stopped me | What would have helped |
|---|---|---|
| What it is, "Mapping takes two things, a pile" | In Geneious I clicked "Map to Reference" and it just happened. Here the first paragraph says LGE records the answer as a BAM, but I do not yet know whether a BAM is something I open or something the app keeps for itself. | Say in the same sentence that you never open the BAM by hand. |
| What it is, "and a score saying how sure" | The score is described but not named. Later the chapter calls it MAPQ and treats it as already introduced. I had to scroll back. | Name it MAPQ here on first mention. |
| What it is, "It also comes out with a companion index" | I could not tell if the `.bai` is a file I have to keep next to the BAM myself. If I copy the BAM somewhere does the index have to come too? | One sentence saying LGE keeps them together and you never move them separately. |
| What it is, "The entry point in the window is a two-part" | This paragraph told me the choice catches people out before it told me what the choice is. I read it twice. | Put the two steps first and the warning after. |
| Why you would do this, "or from a repeat that appears a million times" | I do not know what a repeat is in this sense. Geneious never made me think about it and it is not in the glossary list at the top. | Gloss "repeat" at first use like the other terms. |
| Why you would do this, "The slice holds 45,574 read pairs" | I could not tell whether a read pair is one read or two until the next clause said 91,148 total. I had to do the arithmetic to be sure. | Say "read pairs, which is two reads each" the first time. |
| Before you start, "Download the files ... from the manual's fixtures" | I clicked the FASTQ file on GitHub and got a page of gibberish and no obvious Download button. The chapter warns about this but only after the URL, and by then I had already clicked. | Put the "click the filename then Download raw file" instruction before the link. |
| Before you start, "Three of the four mappers arrive in the" | I did not know whether installing the plugin pack takes ten seconds or twenty minutes, or whether it needs the internet. I stopped to worry. | Say roughly how long it takes and that it downloads. |
| Before you start, "No Docker Desktop is needed for any" | Docker Desktop is mentioned only to say it is not needed. I did not know what it was and now I wonder whether other chapters will need it. | Drop it or gloss it. |
| Procedure, "The wizard has five sections stacked" | The section is called Preset for one mapper and Mode for the other three. I picked minimap2 so I saw Preset, but I kept scanning for a Mode section that was not there. | Say plainly that you see one or the other, never both. |
| Procedure, "Select more than one read bundle before" | I do not know what a "read bundle" is. The numbered step below calls the sidebar item a read bundle too, so I guessed it is the FASTQ pair after import, but I was guessing. | Gloss "read bundle" at first use. |
| Procedure step 4, "and it stops guessing the moment you" | I could not tell what this means in practice. Does the wizard change my choice back if I switch reference? Does it remember across runs? | Say what visibly happens on screen. |
| Procedure, "then `samtools view` to drop unwanted records" | Unwanted by whose decision? I have not chosen anything yet at this point in the chapter. | Say the Advanced Settings choices decide what gets dropped. |
| Procedure, "The mapper writes a SAM file that the filter" | A SAM appears here with no explanation, in a chapter that carefully explained BAM. I did not know if it is a third file I will find somewhere. | One clause saying a SAM is the uncompressed text form of a BAM. |
| Procedure, "named for the mapper and the time it ran" | I could not tell whether I am supposed to ever open this folder or just know it exists. | Say whether you ever need to go in there. |
| Settings, "which are the same sheet with a different header" | I read this twice. I thought it meant the four wizards were literally one window and I would have to check which mapper I had picked. | Simplify to say the four wizards look and behave the same. |
| Settings Preset, "Assembly-to-assembly, Spliced CDS/cDNA" | Four of the six preset names mean nothing to me, CDS and cDNA in particular. I could not tell what would happen if I chose wrong. | Gloss CDS and cDNA, or say plainly that only Short-read applies to Illumina. |
| Settings Run Mode, "This setting has no command-line flag" | The reason given is a sentence about how the CLI treats inputs, and I do not use the CLI, so I could not judge whether this matters to me. | Say this note is safe to skip if you are not using commands. |
| Settings, "The next five settings sit inside the collapsed" | The five headings are written as "ID (--rg-id)." with the flag in the title, which made me think I had to type a flag somewhere in the window. | Keep the flag out of the heading, it is already in the last sentence. |
| Settings Platform, "so a short-read preset writes `ILLUMINA`" | I could not tell whether these are values I would ever see on screen or only inside the file. | Say where in the window the value is visible. |
| Settings Threads, "It defaults to the number of processor cores" | I do not know how many cores my Mac has and the chapter does not say where to find out. It mentions 14 as if I would recognise mine. | Say the wizard already fills in the right number for your machine. |
| Settings Secondary alignments, "with Bowtie2 adding `-k 10` and BBMap" | These flags mean nothing to me and I could not tell if I ever have to type them. | Say the app adds them for you. |
| Settings Supplementary, "and note that the command-line flag is the inverse" | I read this three times to work out whether the box being ticked keeps or drops things. It says the box is on by default and that ticking it keeps them, which describes ticking a box that is already ticked. | State it once, ticked keeps them and unticked drops them. |
| Settings Min mapping quality, "since 60 is the practical ceiling" | I do not know why 60 is a ceiling, or why the range goes to 60 if nothing reaches above it. It reads like a coincidence. | Say the mappers themselves never emit a score above 60. |
| Settings Extra arguments, "a parse error both blocks Run and prints" | I do not know what a parse error looks like. I would not recognise one. | Describe what appears, for example red text in the footer. |
| Import section, "works out which reference assembly the file names from its own header" | I read this sentence four times. "the file names from its own header" parsed as a list of filenames. | Rephrase so "names" is not read as a noun. |
| Reading the results, "Est. Coverage is the estimated average depth" | Depth is used here before it is defined, then defined two paragraphs later as the number of reads covering an average position. I looked for the definition and gave up. | Define depth at this first mention. |
| Reading the results, "which is 44.7, which is what a human genome" | I could not tell whether 44.7 is good because it is high or good because it hit a target. The next sentence says under 10 is too thin, which leaves a large gap I cannot judge. | Give the usable range, not just the fixture value and the floor. |
| Flag Stats, "by tallying the flag bits on every record" | Flag bits are new here and never explained. I do not know what a flag is on a read. | Gloss "flag" as the small set of yes or no labels on each record. |
| Flag Stats, "which is 99.19% of the paired reads" | I could not reproduce this number. 90,414 out of 91,148 is 99.2%, but the text says "of the paired reads" and I did not know which listed count that is. | Name the denominator. |
| Mapper comparison, the "Median MAPQ" column | This number appears only in the table. The Inspector list earlier does not include it, so I do not know where I would see it for my own run. | Say where median MAPQ is found. |
| Mapper comparison, "because the four programs scale that confidence score differently" | If the scales differ, I do not know how to compare Bowtie2's 42 to the Min mapping quality of 20 suggested earlier. That felt important and unanswered. | One sentence saying whether the threshold means the same thing across mappers. |
| What good looks like, "which is expected in shotgun sampling of a pathogen" | Shotgun, pathogen, and classification all arrive at once in a chapter that otherwise used human data. I lost the thread of the troubleshooting list. | Keep the human example, or move this aside into a note. |
| What good looks like, "A run where the Operations panel reports a pairing failure" | I do not know what a pairing failure looks like in that panel. Is it a red row? A message? | Say what you would actually see. |
| What good looks like, "read the sidecar's version fields" | I do not know what a sidecar is or how to open one. Provenance is glossed but sidecar is not. | Say where the sidecar is and how to view it. |
| Reading the results, "Every mapping run also writes a `mapping-result.json`" | I was told this file holds coverage breadth, but not how to open it or whether I am meant to. It is the only place the chapter gives that number. | Say how to view it, or show breadth in the Inspector. |
| On the command line, `--bundle "$HOME/Desktop/lge-docs/..."` | This path names a project called "LGE Manual Demo" that appears nowhere else in the chapter. I thought I had missed a step where I named my project. | Use the same project name the Before you start section implies. |

One thing I learned: reads are useless on their own because they have no address, and mapping is what gives each one an address so they can be stacked into evidence.

One thing I still could not do: judge whether my own run was good. The chapter gives one fixture's numbers and one floor value for depth, but not a range I could hold my own result against, and it never tells me where to find median MAPQ or coverage breadth for a run I made myself.

The sentence I liked most: "When forty reads all sit over the same position and thirty-eight of them read A where the reference reads G, that is evidence of a real difference between your sample and the reference rather than one instrument error."
