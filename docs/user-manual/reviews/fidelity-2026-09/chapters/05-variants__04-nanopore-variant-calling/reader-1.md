# Reader report: Nanopore Variant Calling

Reader 1. Sophomore, one genetics course, no lab time, never opened a terminal.

| Location | What stopped me | What would have helped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is / "Oxford Nanopore reads carry a different..." | I do not know what "Oxford Nanopore" is as a thing. Is it a company, a machine, or a file type? The chapter starts as if I already own one. | One sentence saying Oxford Nanopore is a company that makes sequencing machines, and that its reads are long and error-prone. |
| What it is / "Run LoFreq or iVar on raw..." | LoFreq and iVar appear with no gloss. I do not learn they are the short-read callers until much later. | Call them "the short-read callers LoFreq and iVar" on first mention. |
| What it is / "so a run of six A..." | I could follow this, but I had to read twice to see that the machine is bad at counting bases, not at identifying them. | Say plainly that the pore can tell which base but not how many. |
| What it is / "and their shape shifts every time..." | "Pore chemistry" is used here and again in Step 4, and it is never glossed. One thing I learned. Nanopore machines are bad at counting repeated bases, and that single fact explains both why these reads need their own variant callers and why the results are full of insertions and deletions rather than substitutions.

One thing I still could not do. Find a valid model name to type into the Medaka Model field, because the only method given is a terminal command and the placeholder shown in the field is deliberately the wrong kind of model.

The sentence I liked most. "Depth is what rescues noisy reads."
