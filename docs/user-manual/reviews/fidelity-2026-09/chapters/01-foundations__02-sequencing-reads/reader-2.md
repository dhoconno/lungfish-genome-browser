# Reader report: Sequencing Reads

Persona: a senior who has pipetted for two years and has never analyzed data. I have made libraries. I have never opened a terminal or a FASTQ file.

| Location | What stopped me | What would have helped |
|---|---|---|
| What it is, "A sequencing read is one fragment" | A read is called "one fragment of DNA that came off a sequencing instrument," but I made fragments at the bench and each one is a physical molecule. Is the read the molecule or the letters? I read this twice. | Say the read is the data the instrument wrote down about a fragment, not the fragment itself. |
| What it is, "Reads arrive in a FASTQ file" | Plain-text is used without gloss. I do not know whether that means I can open it in Word. | Gloss plain-text once as a file of ordinary characters any text editor can show. |
| What it is, "This chapter takes those three in turn" | Fixture is used here and everywhere after, and never explained. I do not know if a fixture is a file, a folder, or a demo dataset. | Gloss fixture at first use as the example dataset shipped with the manual. |
| What it is, "The HG002 chromosome 20 slice" | Slice is not defined. I assumed a physical slice of tissue at first, which is what slice means in my lab. | Say the slice is a small region cut out of the data, not the sample. |
| Why you would do this, "A variant call is a claim" | Variant call, assembly, and classification all appear in three sentences with no gloss. I know what a variant is in a paper, not what calling one means. | Gloss variant call at first use as deciding a position differs from the reference. |
| Why you would do this, "a well-characterised human genome from" | Genome in a Bottle is named as if I should know it. I do not. | One clause saying it is a reference project that supplies known-answer human samples. |
| Why you would do this, "Reads for both fixtures are cited" | The line renders as an unfilled template placeholder on the page, with braces and a pipe character. That is not English and I could not tell if it was a typo or something I was supposed to click. | This looks like a placeholder that leaked into the text. |
| Before you start, "choose File > New Project (Cmd-N)" | This is the only place a keystroke appears, and I could not tell whether Cmd-N is required or just the shortcut for the menu item. | State that either route does the same thing. |
| Before you start, "Download the files HG002.chr20.10.0-10.5Mb.R1" | I am told to download and remember where I saved them, then told importing is covered later. So I have two files and no instruction, and I could not do the viewport section that comes later. | Say plainly that the files are only needed once you reach the import chapter. |
| Before you start, "Nothing here needs a plugin pack" | Plugin pack and Docker Desktop are both named without gloss. I do not know what either is or whether I was supposed to install one. | Drop both names or gloss them in half a sentence each. |
| The four-line FASTQ record, "Here is the second record in" | The header says the file is `.fastq.gz`, and a later section says gz is compressed. The record is shown as readable text anyway, and I could not tell how you got readable text out of a compressed file. | Say the record is shown as it appears after decompression. |
| The four-line FASTQ record, "its colon-separated fields name the instrument" | Six things are named for a header that shows seven colon-separated fields, and flow cell versus run versus lane versus tile all blur together. I counted fields twice and gave up. | Label the fields on the illustration instead of listing them in prose. |
| The four-line FASTQ record, "occasionally N, the letter for" | I did not know whether an N counts against my read quality or is separate from the quality score. | One clause saying an N carries a very low quality score. |
| The four-line FASTQ record, "because a quality character can look" | This explains why the separator exists, and it is the first hint that quality characters are letters too. That surprised me before the Phred section explained it. | Move this reassurance next to the Phred decoding. |
| Compressed FASTQ files, "hold 22,662,846 bases between them" | I do not know how to judge this number. Is 22 million bases a lot for one sample? Nothing tells me. | Compare it to something, such as the size of the region sequenced. |
| Compressed FASTQ files, "so a trimmed or filtered bundle can be" | Trimmed, filtered, and bundle all appear before any of them is defined. Bundle in particular gets used constantly later. | Gloss bundle at first use as how LGE groups the files of one sample. |
| Paired-end reads, "The SRA convention writes the suffix" | SRA is an acronym with no expansion anywhere in the chapter. | Expand it once. |
| Paired-end reads, "the two records printed below carry the same header" | The R1 read is 250 bases and the R2 read is 249. Same fragment, same header, different length, and nothing says why. I assumed I had misread it. | One clause saying trimming or the instrument can leave mates unequal in length. |
| Paired-end reads, "a variant caller counts a pair as one observation" | I could not follow why counting a pair twice would be dishonest. Two reads did report the base. | Say the two mates are not independent evidence because they came from one molecule. |
| Paired-end reads, "Split the pair, lose one file, or reorder one" | I am warned that this degrades quietly, and given no way to check that my own files are still in step. | Point to whatever in LGE flags a mismatched pair. |
| When the mates overlap, "which is common in amplicon libraries" | Amplicon library is used before the amplicon chapter. I have made libraries but do not know the analysis meaning here. | Point forward to the amplicon chapter at this word. |
| When the mates overlap, "LGE's Merge Overlapping Pairs operation uses bbmerge" | `bbmerge` is named without saying what it is. It reads like a command I am supposed to type. | Say it is the underlying tool LGE runs for you. |
| Phred quality scores, "It is defined as Q = -10 * log10(P)" | The formula came before the plain-language version. I stopped at the formula, and only the table after it made sense. | Put the table first and the formula after it. |
| Phred quality scores, "Q40 Routine on modern Illumina instruments" | The table's practice column says Q30 is the working definition of a good base and Q40 is routine. If Q40 is routine, I do not know why Q30 is the bar. | Say what threshold to actually use. |
| Phred quality scores, "take its ASCII code and subtract 33" | ASCII is never glossed, and I have no way to look up a character's ASCII code. The four anchors given are the only ones I could use. | Gloss ASCII as the numbering computers give each character. |
| Phred quality scores, "so those five bases are Q35, about a 1-in-3,000" | I could not get from Q35 to 1 in 3,000 with the table given, which only lists round tens. I tried and failed. | Say the value falls between the Q30 and Q40 rows. |
| Phred quality scores, "the tools underneath it, fastp, BWA-MEM2, and minimap2" | Three tool names dropped with no explanation of what each does. I do not know if I have to install them. | Say LGE ships and runs these and you never call them yourself. |
| Read length and platform differences, "Q12 to Q20 simplex, Q30+ duplex" | Simplex and duplex appear only here and are never defined. | Gloss both in one clause, or drop the distinction. |
| Read length and platform differences, "Ion Torrent 200 to 400 bp" | Ion Torrent is in the table and never mentioned again. I could not tell if LGE supports it. | Say whether LGE handles it. |
| A nanopore read, "The header is a plain UUID rather than" | UUID is not glossed. | Gloss it as a randomly generated unique name. |
| A nanopore read, "Q7, roughly a 1-in-5 chance of being wrong" | This section says a Q7 nanopore read is normal, and the What good looks like section says the same fixture's Q7.9 is below range. Those two read as contradicting each other. | Reconcile the two statements about the same number. |
| A nanopore read, "reach an N50 of 10,615 bases, meaning half" | N50 is glossed in a trailing clause I had to read three times. Half the bases sit in reads at least that long is not a sentence I can hold. | Give N50 its own sentence with a small worked example. |
| A HiFi read, "which decodes to Q93" | Q93 is off the end of every scale in the chapter, and I could not judge whether that is real. The text says it is consensus confidence, which helped, but I still do not know if I should trust a Q93. | Say plainly that Q93 does not mean a 1-in-2-billion error rate in practice. |
| How LGE shows a read set, "Click a FASTQ bundle in the sidebar" | I never imported anything, because the import chapter comes later, so I could not do any of this section. I read it as description, not as steps. | Mark the whole section as read-only until after the import chapter. |
| How LGE shows a read set, "The summary bar carries nine cards" | Nine cards are named across one paragraph with no list or table, and I lost track after the fifth. | A small table of card name and meaning. |
| How LGE shows a read set, "GC is the percentage of bases that are G or C" | GC content is given a definition but no interpretation. I do not know what GC value would worry me. | One clause on what a normal GC looks like for human. |
| How LGE shows a read set, "It loads the first 1,000 records" | I could not tell whether the summary cards are also computed from only 1,000 records or from everything. | Say the cards use the whole bundle and only the table is windowed. |
| What good looks like, "Judge a read set on four numbers" | Four numbers are promised, then coverage turns out not to be a number I can get from the FASTQ at all. So I really have three. | Say up front that the fourth comes only after mapping. |
| What good looks like, "a viral amplicon sample needs a few hundred thousand" | Two wildly different targets are given, hundreds of millions and a few hundred thousand, with no way to work out where my own sample sits. | Give the rule that connects read count to genome size. |
| What good looks like, "a minimum of 50" | A minimum read length of 50 in a set of 250-base reads is not explained. I could not tell if that is a problem. | Say short reads in the tail are expected after trimming. |
| What good looks like, "gives a mean depth of 44.7x with 99.77%" | Depth and coverage are used as if interchangeable, and the x suffix is never explained. | Say depth and coverage mean the same thing here, and gloss the x. |
| What good looks like, "which is comfortable for calling small variants" | Small variants is used with no gloss, and I do not know what a large one would be. | Gloss small variants once. |
| What good looks like, "ask your sequencing provider before going further" | Good advice, but nothing tells me what to send them or what to ask. | Name the two or three numbers to quote. |

The one thing I learned: a FASTQ file is four lines per read and nothing else, and the fourth line has exactly one character for every letter on the second.

The one thing I still could not do: open the HG002 files I was told to download, because importing is deferred to a later chapter, so the entire viewport section stayed theoretical.

The sentence I liked most: "One read is a short, noisy guess at what one piece of your sample said."
