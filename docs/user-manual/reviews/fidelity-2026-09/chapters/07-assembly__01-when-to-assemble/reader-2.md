Three lines.

The one thing I learned. Two assemblers given the same reads can both be right and still disagree, and on a circular genome the longer answer is usually an overlap the assembler failed to trim.

The one thing I still could not do. Decide whether my own sample needs assembling, because every decision rule in the chapter asks for a number I do not have, percent identity to a reference, expected genome size, or my coverage.

The sentence I liked most. "De novo" is Latin for "from new", and it is the whole point.
