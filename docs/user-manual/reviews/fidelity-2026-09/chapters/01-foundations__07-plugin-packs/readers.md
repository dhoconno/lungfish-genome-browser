## Summary

The most common failure across all four readers was undefined vocabulary at first use, seen in command-line tools, trimming, integrity checks, volumes, hexadecimal strings, and units dropped after a number. The second most common failure was information given in the wrong order, where a term or fact such as locally-built databases, the pinned dependency list, or the lineage-data mention is used well before the chapter explains it, or where a stray repeated sentence about experimental packs left every reader re-reading the same passage. The third was numbers readers could not act on, including sizes, memory figures, and compression tradeoffs stated without a way to check their own machine or judge the cost, most visibly around the Kraken2 database table and the SSD and memory requirements in Before you start.
