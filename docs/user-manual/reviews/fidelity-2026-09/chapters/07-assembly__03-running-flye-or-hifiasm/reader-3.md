The one thing I learned. A repeat creates a fork in the assembly graph, and a read long enough to span the repeat resolves it, which is why long reads give fewer and longer contigs.

The one thing I still could not do. I could not judge any number in this chapter on my own. Coverage, N50, contig count, and percent shortfall are all described with words like thin, small, far under, and well-covered, but almost never with a threshold I could compare my own result against.

The sentence I liked most. "A contig is a hypothesis about what the genome looks like, assembled from reads that carry their own errors, and nothing in the assembly checked it against an external truth."
