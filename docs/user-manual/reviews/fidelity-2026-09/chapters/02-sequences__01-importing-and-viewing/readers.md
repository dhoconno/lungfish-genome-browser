# Reader reports, merged, 02-sequences/01-importing-and-viewing

Four undergraduate-level readers reviewed this chapter independently. This table merges their findings by location, treating rows as the same finding when they name the same sentence or the same gap, even when the wording differs.

| Location | What stopped readers | Readers hit | Shortest fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What it is, "indexes" | The word index is used two or three times before anything says what it means. | 4 | Gloss index at first use as a lookup table for jumping to a position. |
| What it is, "A GFF3, GTF, or BED file" | Three formats arrive at once, GTF and BED are never glossed the way GFF3 is, and readers cannot tell whether the differences matter. | 4 | Say in one clause that all three are feature-only text formats. |
| Why you would do this, HBB record size and gene count | Readers expect HBB to be one small gene. The record is 81,706 bases and holds eight genes, and nothing says this is a gene cluster before that number lands. | 4 | Name the cluster and say it groups several related genes. |

## Summary

The most common failure is a term or number introduced and used before it is ever explained, examples include index, the GFF3/GTF/BED trio, the codon tables, and the ORF defaults. A close second is instructions and warnings placed in the wrong order, such as the version suffix warning landing after the example that needed it. The third recurring pattern is a forward or sideways reference to material the chapter never delivers, such as the operations dialog, stacked-sequence views, and the CDS transfer workflow.
